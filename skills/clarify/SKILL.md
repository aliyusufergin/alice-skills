---
name: clarify
description: Turn a rough idea into a shared understanding of what the user wants, including what they meant but did not say. Use when starting something whose intent is still vague or only partly stated.
---

Find out what the user wants to be true when this is done. The goal is their intent, not a list of decisions.

Learn what you can from the project and the request first. Facts are your job; intent and preferences are theirs.

Say the intent back wider than they stated it, including what they probably want but did not say and what they would rule out. A concrete guess is faster to correct than an open question is to answer. Name what you take to matter most, since that decides what comes first. Keep it short enough to read at a glance.

Ask only when the answer changes what gets made and you can neither discover it nor cheaply reverse it later. Put each question with your recommended answer, all in one round. Take a further round only when an answer opens a new question that passes the same test. Everything else is your call: name it as an assumption so it stays easy to change.

You are done when you can state the intent in a few sentences and the user agrees. Then recommend where to go next, and let them choose: work that fits in one session can simply be done and saved as a checkpoint, and work that spans sessions goes to the spec skill. Either way, the survey skill comes first when the approach is open or the territory unfamiliar.
